# Balance bike (the model we use)

This model derives its visible geometry from the original
[`Balance_bike.usdz`](source/Balance_bike.usdz). It is the green bike shown by the
viewer by default and the one every scene and the seated K1 use. The old blue
[placeholder](../bike_primitive/README.md) remains available with `--model bike_primitive`.

| File | What |
|---|---|
| `bike.xml` | MuJoCo model: frame, fork with steering hinge, two wheel hinges, collision proxies |
| `meshes/` | Visual meshes (OBJ) and the tyre collision hulls |
| `measurements.json` | Resulting dimensions and each component's body assignment |
| `source/` | The original Sketchfab USDZ and its attribution (CC BY 4.0) |

`bike.xml`, `meshes/` and `measurements.json` are generated by `scripts/convert_bike_usdz.py`
from `source/`; regenerate them rather than editing by hand.

## Scale and physical properties

One uniform scale makes the wheel centres **0.565 m** apart, using the project
owner's approximate measurement. The source is also reoriented into an x-forward,
y-left, z-up frame, with level axles and the origin on the floor under the rear
axle. The stem is then extended along its steering axis to put the grip centres
at **0.45 m**: 3.99 cm upward and 1.59 cm rearward (4.30 cm along the axis).
The bar and grips move together without changing their shape or spacing; the
short stem section above the headset is lengthened. The saddle, bearings, lower
fork and wheels retain their uniformly scaled geometry. This requested adjustment
does not establish the real bike's available stem adjustment range.

Resulting dimensions and every component's body assignment are recorded in
[`measurements.json`](measurements.json). See the separate
[physical-bike measurements](../../docs/bike-dimensions.md) for comparison.

The asset had no authored physics. This derivative adds a free frame, steering
hinge, two wheel hinges, gravity, and collision proxies. Its **1.5 kg total mass
is provisional**, based on the owner's unweighed 1–2 kg estimate. Component mass
fractions retain the original placeholder's ratios; frame/fork inertias are
inferred from the mass-bearing collision primitives, while wheel inertias
assume a uniform torus. These are documented assumptions, not calibration data.

Visible meshes use zero mass and disable collision. Group 2 contains visuals;
group 3 contains collision proxies, hidden by default in the native viewer.
The rounded tyre proxy is a convex hull; filling the central hub opening is
intentional. The frame's concavity is approximated by several primitives rather
than a single solid convex hull. Friction, damping and steering limits remain
unmeasured. This file has no robot, clip connections or balancing controller; the seated
scene adds the robot and the hand-clip welds (`src/k1bike/seat.py`, see
[Seated K1](../../docs/bike-dimensions.md#seated-k1)). The collision proxies are what the
robot touches, so update them whenever the bike geometry changes.

## Reproduction

Linux and macOS, from the repository root (no GPU or viewer required):

```bash
uv run --with usd-core python scripts/convert_bike_usdz.py
```

`usd-core` is only needed to regenerate the meshes and MJCF. The normal viewer
and tests load the generated files using the project's pinned MuJoCo package.
The conversion is specific to the source hash in the manifest; changing to a
different USD export requires inspecting its components and body assignments.
The owner's 56.5 cm wheelbase and 45 cm grip-centre height, plus the provisional
1.5 kg mass, are built-in defaults. No dimension arguments are required;
`--wheelbase`, `--handlebar-height` and `--mass` are optional overrides.
